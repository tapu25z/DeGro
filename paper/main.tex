\title{DeGro: Target Determinacy Verification and Grounded Repair for Executable Mathematical Reasoning}
\author{Anonymous Author(s)}

\begin{abstract}
Executable mathematical reasoning can run successfully while silently solving an underconstrained or mistranslated problem.  Existing execution checks expose syntax, runtime, and arithmetic failures, but do not establish that the encoded constraints determine the quantity requested by the source.  We introduce \emph{DeGro}, a neuro-symbolic framework that reframes execution verification as \emph{target determinacy diagnosis}.  DeGro asks a solver for two feasible assignments that disagree on the requested target.  If such a pair exists, a grounded repair policy must either add a source-supported constraint that restores determinacy or abstain; unsupported assumptions are never admitted as repairs.  In a preregistered paired evaluation that fixes the formalization while varying only source support, DeGro improves repair-or-abstain accuracy for GPT-OSS 20B but not for Gemma 4 31B.  Two fully human-verified natural-error cohorts show a complementary pattern: DeGro significantly improves accuracy on the supported MATH-500 cohort, whereas the higher-coverage ALG514 study exposes answer-compatible formalization errors.  Detection is substantially easier than repair on heterogeneous natural errors, and concrete witness assignments add no measurable benefit beyond the determinacy verdict.  These results establish target determinacy as an auditable intermediate guardrail---not a proof of source-to-formal semantic faithfulness.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Large language models increasingly delegate mathematical computation to programs and symbolic solvers.  Program-aided reasoning reduces arithmetic errors, while systems such as SymCode generate self-contained symbolic programs and repair execution failures~\cite{gao2023pal,nezhad2026symcode}.  Yet successful execution is a weak certificate: it shows that an artifact runs, not that the artifact encodes enough of the source problem to justify the requested answer.

Consider: ``Ten tasks are divided between A and B. A receives two more than B. How many does A receive?''  A generated model containing only $a+b=10$ is executable and satisfiable, but $(a,b)=(4,6)$ and $(6,4)$ imply different values of the target $a$.  The same model is faithful for the shorter source ``Ten tasks are divided between A and B. How many does A receive?''  In the first case, $a=b+2$ is a missing translation; in the second, adding it fabricates information.  The solver state is identical, but the correct action changes with source support.

Most verification pipelines observe only execution success, answer agreement, or a scalar confidence signal.  These signals can reject an obvious failure, but they do not expose whether the feasible region uniquely determines the requested target, nor whether a proposed repair is licensed by the source.  This gap matters because a fluent agent can confidently return a number from a model that permits several incompatible answers.

We propose \method{}, a neuro-symbolic framework that treats this failure as structured diagnosis.  It constructs two renamed copies of the generated specification and asks whether both copies can satisfy the constraints while disagreeing on the target.  A satisfying query yields auditable evidence of ambiguity.  DeGro then separates two causes of that ambiguity through a source-grounded repair-or-abstain gate: repair a supported omission, or decline to invent the missing fact.

\takeaway{DeGro is not a general proof of semantic correctness.  It is a target-directed diagnostic layer that turns one silent failure---satisfiable code with an ambiguous answer---into a checkable repair-or-abstain decision.}

Our main contributions are:

\noindent\textbf{(1) A target-centric view of executable verification.}
We formalize target determinacy as agreement on the requested expression across all feasible assignments.  This is strictly weaker than full model uniqueness and directly matches what an answering agent needs to justify.

\noindent\textbf{(2) An evidence-grounded diagnosis-and-repair pipeline.}
We introduce a two-copy solver query that returns target-divergent models and a deterministic gate that accepts only source-supported, consistent, non-redundant repairs that restore determinacy.

\noindent\textbf{(3) A paired causal evaluation of repair versus abstention.}
Our preregistered design holds the initial formalization and solver verdict fixed while varying only whether the source contains the missing fact.  It tests decision quality without allowing ambiguity alone to reveal the label.

\noindent\textbf{(4) Human-verified evidence on natural formalization errors.}
MATH-500 and ALG514 cohorts separate target-critical omissions from wrong targets, wrong constraints, and answer-compatible semantic failures.  The results reveal model-dependent gains and a large detection--repair gap.

We ask whether determinacy information improves repair-or-abstain accuracy (\textbf{RQ1}), whether grounding reduces unsupported additions (\textbf{RQ2}), whether concrete witnesses help beyond a bare verdict (\textbf{RQ3}), and how coverage, detection, and repair behave on natural errors (\textbf{RQ4}).

\section{Related Work}
We organize prior work by the feedback it provides: execution signals, learned judgments, formal evidence, or missing-information actions.  DeGro differs in returning a target-specific symbolic diagnosis whose admissible action depends on source support.

\subsection{Executable Mathematical Reasoning}
Chain-of-thought elicits intermediate natural-language reasoning~\cite{wei2022cot}, while PAL and Program-of-Thoughts delegate computation to generated programs~\cite{gao2023pal,chen2023pot}.  Toolformer and ReAct integrate external tools~\cite{schick2023toolformer,yao2023react}; MathCoder and ToRA specialize this pattern to mathematical code and tool interaction~\cite{wang2024mathcoder,gou2024tora}.  SymCode treats an executable symbolic program as the reasoning artifact and repairs runtime failures~\cite{nezhad2026symcode}.  DeGro addresses the complementary case in which code executes but its feasible region does not determine the query.

\subsection{Outcome, Process, and Self-Verification}
Verifier-based selection ranks candidates using learned outcome or process signals~\cite{cobbe2021verifiers,uesato2022feedback,lightman2023verify}.  Self-verification, Self-Refine, and CRITIC ask a model to inspect or revise its output, sometimes with tool feedback~\cite{weng2023selfverification,madaan2023selfrefine,gou2024critic}; intrinsic self-correction can fail without reliable external feedback~\cite{huang2024selfcorrect}.  These methods can improve answer accuracy while still accepting a runnable program that omits a necessary source condition.  DeGro returns a solver-checkable failure state rather than a score or free-form critique.

\subsection{Relational Verification and Solver Diagnosis}
Self-composition reduces a two-execution property to ordinary safety checking by composing a program with a renamed copy~\cite{barthe2004selfcomposition,terauchi2005safety}.  Product programs and Cartesian Hoare logic make relational proofs modular~\cite{barthe2011product,sousa2016cartesian}, while hyperproperties generalize reasoning from single traces to sets of traces~\cite{clarkson2010hyperproperties,clarkson2014hyperltl,finkbeiner2015modelchecking}.  DeGro uses the same two-copy idea, but checks agreement on a mathematical target across feasible assignments rather than noninterference across executions.

ReLoop perturbs parameters to expose semantically incorrect optimization models that still execute~\cite{lian2026reloop}.  SymDiag performs step-level satisfiability and entailment checks and returns structured evidence~\cite{cui2026symdiag}; minimal-core-guided repair targets overconstrained, unsatisfiable formalizations~\cite{sarkar2026cores}.  DeGro instead diagnoses the underconstrained regime: satisfiable specifications with target-divergent models.  Like counterexample-guided verification and synthesis~\cite{clarke2000cegar,solarlezama2006cegis}, it uses a mechanical refutation to guide a bounded next action, but the witness establishes ambiguity rather than the correct repair.

\subsection{Selective Action and Missing Information}
Selective classification formalizes the risk--coverage tradeoff of a reject option~\cite{elyaniv2010foundations,geifman2017selective,cortes2016rejection}; learning-to-defer routes difficult cases to an expert~\cite{mozannar2020defer}, and conformal risk control calibrates selected actions under stated assumptions~\cite{angelopoulos2024conformal}.  Language-model self-knowledge and semantic uncertainty provide confidence signals~\cite{kadavath2022know,kuhn2023semantic}.  DeGro derives abstention from a different condition: the source does not license a constraint needed to determine the target.  Accordingly, \textsc{unknown} and \textsc{unsupported} remain coverage failures rather than proofs.

\subsection{Semantic Faithfulness and Autoformalization}
Classical diagnosis identifies faulty assumptions, while abductive inference searches for hypotheses that restore a desired property~\cite{reiter1987diagnosis,dillig2013abduction}.  SAT-based model repair changes a transition system until it satisfies a property~\cite{attie2008modelrepair}, and LLM-based work repairs declarative Alloy specifications~\cite{alhanahnah2024alloyrepair}.  Our gate is narrower: it adds only a cited, non-redundant, consistency-preserving constraint that restores target determinacy.

Autoformalization translates informal mathematics into formal statements~\cite{wu2022autoformalization,jiang2024mma}.  Benchmarks such as miniF2F, ProofNet, Lean Workbook, and FormalMATH expand the evaluation substrate~\cite{zheng2022minif2f,azerbayev2023proofnet,ying2024leanworkbook,yu2025formalmath}; theorem-proving systems further improve formal reasoning~\cite{yang2023leandojo,azerbayev2024llemma,jiang2023draft,xin2024deepseekprover}.  Yet prover acceptance does not establish that the formal object preserves source meaning.  MIRA-Math explicitly studies requests for a hidden necessary fact~\cite{albateh2026mira}; we derive paired cases from its validated latent states, but evaluate grounded representation repair rather than information exchange.

\section{Methodology}
DeGro is a two-stage framework: it first diagnoses whether a generated specification determines the requested target, then converts an ambiguous verdict into either a source-grounded repair or a principled abstention.  Figure~\ref{fig:workflow} summarizes the complete loop.

\subsection{Problem Setup and Outputs}
Let a natural-language problem be $P$.  A model produces $F=(V,D,C,q)$, where $V$ are variables, $D$ their domains, $C$ constraints, and $q(V)$ the requested expression.  Define
\[
\mathrm{Sol}(F)=\{m\mid m\models D\land C\}.
\]
The target is \emph{determinate under $F$} iff
\[
\forall m_1,m_2\in\mathrm{Sol}(F),\quad q(m_1)=q(m_2).
\]
This is weaker than solution uniqueness: $x=5,y\geq0$ has many models, but target $q=x$ is determinate.

Construct renamed copies $F^{(1)}$ and $F^{(2)}$ and query
\begin{equation}
\Phi(F,q)=F^{(1)}\land F^{(2)}\land q(V^{(1)})\neq q(V^{(2)}).\label{eq:query}
\end{equation}
If \eqref{eq:query} is satisfiable, its two projected models form a \emph{target-divergent witness pair}.  If it is unsatisfiable and $F$ itself is satisfiable, the target is determinate.

\textbf{Guarantee.} For a sound solver and supported theory, an \textsc{unsat} result for Eq.~\eqref{eq:query}, together with $\mathrm{SAT}(F)$, implies that all models of $F$ agree on $q$.  This follows immediately because a disagreeing pair would satisfy Eq.~\eqref{eq:query}.  A timeout or \textsc{unknown} result proves nothing and is never mapped to determinacy.

\paragraph{Soundness boundary.} The guarantee is relative to $F$.  It does not establish $F\equiv P$.  An unsupported extra constraint may make $q$ determinate while mistranslating the source.  Provenance gates reduce, but do not formally eliminate, this semantic gap.

\subsection{Target Determinacy and Soundness}
Target determinacy is query-relative.  A scheduling model can leave individual assignments free while determining total cost; requiring full model uniqueness would reject such a valid answer.  A returned witness pair is independently checkable by substituting both assignments into $D\land C$ and comparing their target values.  Moreover, determinacy is monotone under consistent strengthening: if every model of $F$ agrees on $q$, then every model of $F\land c$ agrees on the same value.  The current policy therefore triggers additions only for ambiguous specifications.  Repairing a determinate but incorrect representation may require deleting or revising an existing constraint and is outside the method.

The converse is unsafe: an arbitrary strengthening can force determinacy.  Adding $a=6$ to $a+b=10$ determines $a$ whether or not the source licenses that fact.  We therefore separate \emph{formal validity}---parseability, consistency, novelty, and sufficiency---from \emph{evidential validity}, which requires source support.

\subsection{Running Example}
For $V=\{a,b\}$, nonnegative integer domains, $C=(a+b=10)$, and $q=a$, the duplicated query is
\[
a_1+b_1=10\land a_2+b_2=10\land a_1\ne a_2.
\]
A solver can return $(4,6)$ and $(6,4)$.  If the source includes ``A receives two more than B,'' then $a=b+2$ is source-supported, consistent, non-redundant, and sufficient; the repaired target is six.  Without that sentence, the same equation would be a fabricated assumption, so the appropriate external action is abstention.  Neither decision exposes the hidden gold answer to the repair model.

\subsection{Framework Overview}
\paragraph{Structured representation.}
The agent emits one typed \textsc{ModelSpec} containing variable sorts and bounds, constraint expressions with source spans, and a target expression.  Deterministic compilers translate the same object to the computational and verification backends.  This avoids divergence between independently generated SymPy and SMT programs.  The initial supported fragment includes integer, real, and Boolean variables; arithmetic equalities and inequalities; finite domains; and selected modular constraints.  Unsupported syntax yields \textsc{not-supported}.

\subsection{Stage I: Target-Determinacy Diagnosis}
After execution, DeGro first tests $\mathrm{SAT}(F)$.  Unsatisfiability yields \textsc{inconsistent}; unknown yields \textsc{unknown}.  Otherwise it evaluates Eq.~\eqref{eq:query}.  Unsatisfiability permits an answer under the current formalization.  Satisfiability yields two assignments with different target values and triggers grounded diagnosis.

This stage is deterministic.  The language model proposes the representation, but it does not judge whether the resulting target is determinate.  The diagnostic record contains the base satisfiability verdict, the two-copy verdict, normalized witness values when available, and any unsupported-theory or timeout state.

\subsection{Stage II: Grounded Repair or Abstention}
The repair model receives $P$, $C$, $q$, and the witness pair, and must return either \textsc{add-constraint} with an exact supporting span and formal expression, or \textsc{abstain}.  A candidate $c$ is accepted only if: (i) it compiles; (ii) its cited span occurs in $P$, or it uses a declared domain-semantics rule; (iii) $F\land c$ is satisfiable; (iv) $F\not\models c$; and (v) rechecking restores determinacy or yields a new witness for the next bounded round.  We use at most two repair rounds.  The external statuses are \textsc{answer}, \textsc{underspecified}, \textsc{inconsistent}, \textsc{unknown}, and \textsc{unsupported}.

Exact substring provenance is deliberately conservative and easy to audit.  It does not prove that a cited sentence was translated correctly; semantic validation of the proposed equation remains a failure mode.

\subsection{Trusted Implementation}
The reference implementation compiles a restricted expression abstract syntax tree to Z3~\cite{demoura2008z3}.  It rejects attribute access, arbitrary function calls, undeclared names, malformed comparisons, and unsupported operators before solver invocation.  Integer, real, and Boolean declarations are typed explicitly; variable bounds compile to constraints.  The two copies use independent variable maps to prevent accidental aliasing, and model values are normalized before entering a diagnostic record.

Grounding is a deployment gate, not only a prompt instruction.  Each repair record contains an action, a formal expression, an exact source span, and an explanation.  The gate checks span membership, recompiles the expression, checks $\mathrm{SAT}(F\land c)$ and $F\not\models c$, and reruns the target-divergence query.  Ablations retain both raw proposals and gated outcomes, so a baseline is not credited under a more permissive acceptance rule.  Every result record stores the initial verdict, proposed action, rejection reason, final verdict, model configuration, and prompt hash.  The trusted computing base comprises the parser, compiler, solver, and deterministic gate; the language model remains untrusted.

\subsection{Repair Acceptance Semantics}
Let $\mathrm{span}(c)$ be the source substring cited for candidate $c$, and let $\mathcal{R}$ be a frozen set of allowed domain-semantics rules.  The gate accepts $c$ exactly when
\begin{align}
\mathrm{Accept}(c) \iff{}& \mathrm{Compiles}(c) \land
  \mathrm{Grounded}(c,P,\mathcal{R}) \nonumber\\
&\land\ \mathrm{SAT}(F\land c) \land \neg(F\models c)
  \land \mathrm{Det}(F\land c,q),
\label{eq:accept}
\end{align}
where grounding requires either exact occurrence of $\mathrm{span}(c)$ in $P$ or a named rule in $\mathcal{R}$.  The policy returns the first accepted candidate within the two-round budget.  If neither round yields an accepted addition, it preserves $F$ and returns \textsc{underspecified}; it never commits a partially validated edit.

This definition prevents three misleading successes.  A redundant restatement cannot receive repair credit because $F\models c$.  A target pin such as $q=6$ cannot pass without source support.  A grounded but insufficient addition cannot pass if target-divergent models remain.  Conversely, the gate deliberately permits a constraint that differs syntactically from $c^*$ when it is source-supported and functionally restores the intended target.

\subsection{Complexity and Failure States}
The determinacy query duplicates variables and constraints once, so its encoding size is linear in the size of $F$ up to the target disequality.  Solver time can still grow nonlinearly with the underlying theory; the implementation therefore preserves timeout and \textsc{unknown} as first-class states.  A determinate input requires no repair-model call.  An ambiguous input incurs at most two calls plus solver rechecks, yielding a bounded agent loop rather than unconstrained self-refinement.

The method distinguishes representation failure from mathematical incompleteness.  \textsc{unsupported} means that the parser or compiler cannot express the attempted formalization.  \textsc{unknown} means that the solver did not decide a supported query.  \textsc{inconsistent} means $F$ has no model.  Only \textsc{underspecified} says that the available source did not support an accepted repair for an ambiguous target.  Collapsing these states would make both coverage and user-facing behavior uninterpretable.

\section{Evaluation}
\subsection{Controlled paired benchmark}
For each gold specification $F^*=\{c_1,\ldots,c_k,c^*\}$ with target-critical fact $c^*$, we create $F^-=F^*\setminus\{c^*\}$.  We retain an item only when $F^*$ is target-determinate, $F^-$ is satisfiable, and $F^-$ admits target-divergent witnesses.  Two cases share exactly $F^-$: in \textsc{omission}, the source $P^+$ contains the fact corresponding to $c^*$; in \textsc{underspecified}, $P^-$ omits it.  The expected decisions are repair and abstain, respectively.  This construction prevents the verifier state alone from revealing the label.

We constructed 120 confirmatory pairs from every remaining compatible MIRA-Math instance after excluding the earlier 80-pair pilot and 100-pair development set~\cite{albateh2026mira}.  The set contains 240 cases from five families.  Selection, source order, prompts, model settings, exclusions, and the dataset hash were frozen before the confirmatory runs.  The cumulative analysis combines these 120 pairs with the earlier 180 pairs and is explicitly secondary.

\paragraph{Integrity checks.}
The builder does not ask an LLM to select or corrupt $c^*$.  It imports the typed missing fact from the MIRA-Math latent state, converts compatible families to \textsc{ModelSpec}, and applies the three solver checks before inclusion.  A deletion that leaves the target unchanged is therefore never mislabeled as the failure mode of interest.  The final subset excludes every earlier pair identifier and exhausts the remaining compatible instances.  Its manifest records source revision \texttt{72ff918}, CC BY 4.0 licensing, selection seed, five family counts, prompt hash, and output hash.

The families exercise distinct constraint structures.  Linear-system separation removes a relation distinguishing otherwise compatible solutions.  Rank-deficient shared systems retain several degrees of freedom.  Graph path sums and Chinese-remainder reconstruction combine discrete equalities with domain restrictions.  Moment problems use coupled polynomial summaries within the supported degree bound.  Family-stratified analysis checks that an easy template does not dominate the aggregate.

\subsection{Methods and hypotheses}
The confirmatory comparison includes \textsc{self-review}, \textsc{grounded self-review}, \textsc{determinacy-guided repair}, and \textsc{DeGro}, which combines determinacy feedback with source grounding and solver-validation gates.  All four methods reuse the same initial formalization and receive the same two-attempt budget.  We ran GPT-OSS 20B and Gemma 4 31B with medium reasoning, temperature 1.0, and a frozen prompt.  All $120\times2\times4=960$ expected records were present for each model; no failed calls enter the confirmatory denominator.

An exploratory decision-stage experiment on the disjoint 100-pair development set also compares minimal witnesses, full target witnesses, and DeGro augmented with full witness assignments.  These variants were introduced after the initial pilot and are not confirmatory.  They address RQ3 by separating the existence of non-uniqueness from the informational content of particular assignments.

The preregistered primary comparison tests whether DeGro improves FDA over grounded self-review.  We test it separately for each model with exact paired McNemar tests and Holm correction across the two tests.  We report the paired percentage-point difference with a pair-cluster bootstrap 95\% interval.  Official model documentation describes configurable reasoning effort and structured output support for GPT-OSS~\cite{openai2025gptoss}.

\subsection{Metrics and analysis}
Primary metrics are repair success rate (RSR), correct abstention rate (CAR), over-repair rate (ORR), and final decision accuracy (FDA).  Unsupported repair (UR) is the fraction of proposed additions that fail the source-support criterion.  Confidence intervals resample pairs, preserving the omission/underspecification coupling.

RSR requires a grounded, functionally correct addition on omission cases.  CAR rewards abstention, rather than an unsupported addition, on underspecified cases.  FDA averages the two balanced case types, and ORR is $1-\mathrm{CAR}$.  We retain separate semantic, functional, and grounded repair fields because a constraint can recover the right number by coincidence or cite a relevant sentence while encoding it incorrectly.  The deployment score uses the grounded outcome.

\subsection{Natural-error study}
To test ecological validity, GPT-OSS 20B generated one Structured SymCode specification per MATH-500 problem without injected errors.  We included every successfully parsed, solver-supported specification with a conclusive verdict in a cohort frozen before annotation or inspection of repair outcomes.  The original problem is the unit of analysis: all methods reuse the same initial specification, preventing shared model generations from being counted as independent observations.

We use AI-assisted annotation followed by full two-person human verification.  GPT-OSS 20B and Gemma 4 31B first proposed MATH-500 labels and evidence under a shared prompt while blinded to the determinacy verdict, witness, repair proposal, and method outcome.  Two reviewers then inspected all 292 Gemma-proposed labels and accepted them as the final MATH-500 annotations.  For ALG514, GPT-OSS 120B proposed 503 labels and re-adjudicated all 22 cases whose semantic status disagreed with verifier-compatible numerical status; two reviewers subsequently inspected and accepted all 503 final labels.  Separate pre-discussion human label files were not retained, so we describe this as full two-person verification of AI-assisted labels rather than independent double annotation and do not report human inter-annotator agreement.  The labels are: correct; missing, wrong, or extra constraint; wrong domain; wrong target; computational error; or other.  Only a source-supported omission that can change the requested target enters the executable-under-formalization subset.

The primary natural-error estimands are ambiguity-detection recall and end-to-end grounded-repair success within that subset.  Secondary measures are repair success conditional on detection, detector precision, false ambiguity and false repair outside the subset, and preservation of adjudicated-correct controls.  Proportions receive Wilson 95\% intervals.  Overall Structured Solver versus DeGro answer accuracy is paired by problem and tested with exact McNemar.  A numerically correct answer is not a successful repair unless an accepted grounded addition restores determinacy.

The MATH-500 cohort contains 292 conclusive, solver-supported formalizations from 500 problems; 208 were excluded by deterministic reasons (65 model-level unsupported, 112 solver-level unsupported, 9 inconsistent, 9 unknown, and 13 call errors).  The auxiliary pre-verification model comparison yielded 67.8\% exact agreement ($\kappa=.458$) and 90.8\% agreement on primary-subset membership, illustrating that the detailed taxonomy is nontrivial.  The ALG514 cohort contains 503 of 514 problems after excluding six solver-unsupported, one model-declared unsupported, and four inconsistent formalizations.  DRAW-1K development results are excluded from the study because its corrected-verifier outcomes and human-final labels were not completed.

\subsection{Error patterns and cross-model behavior}

\section{Validity and Analysis Choices}
\subsection{Why the paired design matters}
A conventional mixture of complete and incomplete questions admits shortcuts: surface wording can reveal missing information, families can differ in difficulty, and a policy can learn that every solver ambiguity should be repaired.  Here, the two cases share latent values, target, domains, retained constraints, and solver verdict.  Only the sentence corresponding to $c^*$ differs.  Pairing therefore removes representation difficulty from the repair-versus-abstain contrast and gives an exact test based on discordant decisions.

The repair policy never receives $c^*$, the gold target, or the gold action.  It receives only the source, current specification, and method-specific diagnostic.  Gold latent state is used after generation for scoring.  Direct target pins are marked unsupported unless licensed by the source and accepted by the same gate.  Raw and gated outcomes reveal when a method appears accurate only because it fabricates a convenient constraint.

\subsection{Internal and statistical validity}
The primary contrast changes only the determinacy signal: both methods receive the same source, specification, schema, grounding requirement, model, and budget.  The final 120 pairs were frozen after excluding all 180 earlier identifiers and before their method outcomes were inspected.  The two model-specific tests and their Holm correction were declared in the manifest.  No family or case is removed based on a repair result.

The cumulative 300-pair analysis is more precise but includes development data observed earlier.  It is therefore labeled secondary throughout.  The 100-pair witness ablation is exploratory because its variants were added after the pilot.  Keeping these roles separate avoids retroactively treating a favorable cumulative result as the confirmatory test.

\subsection{Construct validity}
FDA weights omission and underspecification equally because every latent item contributes one of each.  This exposes the cost of over-repair, but deployments may assign a larger penalty to fabricated facts than to missed repairs.  The released case-level records permit alternative utilities.  UR is conditioned on proposing an addition and must be read with RSR and CAR: a method that always abstains has zero unsupported additions and zero repair utility.

Exact-span provenance is an auditable proxy, not semantic entailment between language and logic.  A model can cite the right sentence and encode the wrong equation.  Conversely, implicit mathematical conventions may justify an expression without a contiguous span.  Separate semantic, functional, grounding, and determinacy fields expose these disagreements instead of collapsing them into numeric answer accuracy.

\subsection{External validity}
MIRA-derived cases are templated and remove exactly one known fact.  They provide causal control but underrepresent paraphrase, irrelevant prose, diagrams, implicit conventions, wrong constraints, and interacting translation errors.  MATH-500 offers more natural language but exposes the opposite limitation: much of it lies outside the present arithmetic SMT fragment.  The evaluations answer different questions and are not merged into a leaderboard score.

Transfer is also bounded.  GPT-OSS 20B and Gemma 4 31B differ in post-training and instruction behavior; their divergent results are direct evidence against a universal prompt claim.  Hosted checkpoints can change, so the artifact records exact model strings, timestamps, reasoning settings, temperature, prompt hashes, and raw responses.

\subsection{Natural-error validity}
The frozen natural-error protocol avoids selecting examples from detector outputs.  Its final MATH-500 and ALG514 labels were fully checked by two human reviewers, but the annotation process began from model proposals and did not preserve separate, blinded pre-discussion human label sets.  We therefore claim human verification, not independent human annotation or inter-annotator reliability.  MATH-500 prevalence is additionally conditional on only 58.4\% solver-fragment coverage; ALG514's 97.9\% coverage provides a less selected but narrower algebraic comparison.  We report the datasets separately and do not pool their prevalence estimates.

This distinction prevents circular validation.  DeGro's ambiguous verdict is hidden from annotators and cannot define the positive class.  A positive case must instead contain a source-supported target-critical omission established from the source and generated specification.  Correct, wrong-constraint, wrong-target, and wrong-domain cases remain in the denominator used to measure false ambiguity, false repair, and preservation.

\section{Agent Integration}
DeGro is a verifier policy inside a larger agent, not a standalone solver.  A host agent first parses the task and produces an executable artifact.  The verifier then returns a typed observation that a controller maps to an action, as summarized in Table~\ref{tab:policy}.  Keeping the states distinct matters: \textsc{unknown} denotes solver inconclusiveness, \textsc{unsupported} denotes a representation limitation, and \textsc{underspecified} is a conclusion about missing source support.

In an interactive setting, \textsc{underspecified} can trigger a clarification request rather than terminate the task.  For the running example, the agent can explain that $(4,6)$ and $(6,4)$ both satisfy the stated total and ask which additional relationship holds.  The controlled benchmark scores abstention because no information channel is available; it does not claim that stopping is preferable to clarification.

The verifier and repair model can also be separated.  One component generates $F$, a deterministic tool checks determinacy, and another component proposes a grounded action.  This reduces self-confirmation and produces an audit trail linking each accepted repair to a source span and two solver checks.  Interfaces should phrase a successful result as ``the encoded constraints determine the target,'' not ``the problem has been formally proved.''

\section{Discussion}
\paragraph{What the evidence supports.}
The paired construction demonstrates that a solver state can be held constant while the correct action changes with the source.  For GPT-OSS 20B, determinacy-guided grounding improves the joint decision and reduces unsupported additions.  The mechanism is most useful as a guard against generic review that fills a formal gap with a plausible but unstated relation.

\paragraph{What the evidence does not support.}
The study does not establish semantic equivalence between $P$ and $F$, a universal benefit from grounding prompts, or an incremental benefit from concrete witnesses.  It also does not show superiority over unrestricted program generation on MATH-500.  The Gemma result and the incomplete broad-benchmark method logs make these boundaries empirical findings rather than boilerplate caveats.

\paragraph{Why the negative witness result matters.}
The two-copy query remains essential for computing the determinacy verdict and can return an auditable counterexample.  Nevertheless, exposing the full pair to the repair model does not improve decisions on the present data.  A simpler deployed policy can therefore communicate only the typed verdict by default and retain witnesses for audit, explanation, or harder cases.  This saves context while preserving the formal check.

\paragraph{Relation to selective action.}
Confidence-based abstention asks whether a predictor appears uncertain.  DeGro asks a different question: whether the encoded constraints logically permit more than one target and whether the source licenses a restriction.  The two signals can be composed.  A determinate target with low language-model confidence may still deserve fallback, while an ambiguous target should not be authorized merely because the model is confident.

\section{Artifact and Reproducibility}
The artifact package contains the typed compiler and two-copy solver query, repair gates, unit tests, dataset builders, frozen manifests, prompts and schemas, raw JSONL records, and analysis scripts.  The confirmatory manifest records the MIRA-Math source revision and license, selection seed, five family counts, exact model strings, decoding settings, prompt SHA-256, and output SHA-256.  Its 120-pair output hash is \texttt{5616d0c6\ldots4ce3f0b}; each model analysis consumes all 960 expected records.

Analysis scripts reconstruct tables from JSONL and fail when expected pair counts are missing.  The cumulative assembler records whether a row originated in the pilot, development, or confirmatory subset.  This permits independent reproduction of the 120-pair headline without incorporating earlier observations.  Statistical code implements pair-cluster bootstrap intervals, exact two-sided McNemar tests, and Holm adjustment.

The natural-error packets and verified labels are separately blinded and content-addressed; their full SHA-256 values are recorded in the artifact manifests.  Detector verdicts, repairs, method names, and answer outcomes are absent from initial annotation inputs.  No credential, provider account identifier, or secret is included in the release.

\section{Limitations and Risks}
Target determinacy is neither semantic faithfulness nor answer correctness relative to the source.  Wrong or extra constraints can force a unique incorrect target and evade the check.  An omission that does not affect $q$ is likewise invisible.  The current SMT fragment excludes much nonlinear, transcendental, and diagrammatic mathematics, so accuracy without coverage is misleading.  Witnesses may differ on irrelevant variables, multiple omissions can exceed the bounded repair budget, and exact-span grounding underhandles implicit mathematical knowledge.

The controlled study removes one known target-critical constraint and therefore estimates a repair decision under idealized formalization control.  Natural errors are more heterogeneous: wrong targets and wrong constraints dominate, while only 24 MATH-500 and eight ALG514 cases are target-critical omissions.  MATH-500 prevalence is conditional on 58.4\% coverage, and its DeGro outcome includes six call errors and one unsupported record as failures.  ALG514 is narrower algebraically, and its one-case accuracy gain is exploratory.  These qualifications prevent the human-verified cohorts from being interpreted as universal prevalence or repair estimates.

The method can increase false confidence if representation-relative determinacy is presented as truth.  Interfaces should expose the constraints and any cited repair rather than display an unqualified certification.  The evaluated datasets contain generated or public mathematical problems and no personal data.  AI assistance used in study design, implementation, experimentation, or writing requires venue-compliant disclosure; authors remain responsible for every claim, citation, and released artifact.

\section{Conclusion}
Execution answers whether an artifact runs; DeGro asks whether its feasible models agree on the quantity the agent is about to report.  Grounded repair-or-abstain then separates a recoverable translation omission from genuinely missing information.  On the preregistered 120-pair set, DeGro improves GPT-OSS 20B by five FDA points but does not improve Gemma 4 31B, ruling out a model-independent gain.  On natural errors it detects most target-critical MATH-500 omissions but rarely repairs them, while ALG514 exposes nine answer-compatible semantic failures.  Concrete witnesses provide no measurable advantage over the verdict-plus-grounding policy.  The resulting claim is narrow but actionable: target determinacy is a checkable intermediate safety condition, and its utility must be reported together with grounding behavior, representation coverage, and the semantic boundary between source and formalization.
